# Rex — art directions before implementation

These are proposed visual treatments, not rendered animations. The story and 56-second editorial timing stay the same across them. Keep the approved Confused logo, red #FF4055, white eyes and the actual dashboard. Excited appears at acknowledgement or a real completion; Confused reads as curious personality, not as a broken/error state.

| Direction | What it looks like | Motion and mascot | Openverse background use | Fit / cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Editorial tech + character — recommended** | Large crisp type, charcoal opening, warm paper product stage, red keyword accents. Real UI gets most of the screen. | Snappy word reveals, gentle window push-ins, eye tracking and a brief Excited reaction. Shallow card depth, no full 3D character required. | City-at-dusk photo in opening; a short India crop near the close. Otherwise restrained solid fields keep UI readable. | Best match for Rex's current identity and root launch-video kinetic guidance. Lowest execution risk. |
| **2. Soft vinyl / studio 3D** | Red mascot feels like a small matte vinyl object; soft studio light, paper surfaces, a dark final hero. No metallic robot or mouth. | Small tilt, buoyant settle, projected eye shapes; UI cards sit on shallow 3D planes. Limit the 3D hero to reveal/outro, preserving readable screen footage. | Photos appear on a distant, softly defocused rear panel; not pasted onto the character. | Most tactile and premium; more modelling, lighting and consistency work. Three.js is a reference, not yet selected as the implementation. |
| **3. Indian editorial photo collage** | Cropped city/street imagery, paper cut-outs, restrained halftone and clean red labels. Preserve real product UI inside clear frames. | Photographic layers slide on different depth planes; mascot's gaze guides attention between research, files and approval. | Openverse photography is a visible part of the hook and 45–48s language beat; quiet/blank fields during product proof. | Stronger local character and visual originality; needs disciplined crops and attribution. Avoid a tourist montage unrelated to the story. |
| **4. Playful graphic / 2D cartoon timing** | Flat red/ink/paper graphics, oversized eyes, tabs that fold into a neat stack. No decorative AI sparkles or invented product UI. | Anticipation, squash/stretch in transitions, eye blinks on semantic changes, minimal elastic overshoot. Return the mark to its approved silhouette on brand holds. | A few graphic-framed photo fragments in opening/close; the middle is mostly vector and actual screens. | Most expressive and friendly; lighter than full 3D. Risk: excessive bounce can make a capable operator feel toy-like. |

Recommendation: **Direction 1**, with a small amount of Direction 3's photography. The product's actual actions should carry the story; character performance makes Rex memorable. Direction 2 is the alternative if the priority is a cinematic mascot hero.

Design grammar from the supplied root skill: one short line per beat; one red pill at most per line; words arrive with the voice; ~100 px caption type at 1080p; hero text around 240–300 px; readable source and approval crops; dark → light → dark progression. UI shots hard-cut or make restrained push-ins; reserve a larger transition for the Rex reveal. Show a fresh visual about every 1.5–4 seconds, with the Hinglish command held for comprehension.

Use the custom Confused mascot mark in place of the skill's default glowing orb. A desktop/web product is better served by browser windows than an invented mobile-phone ad. No style has been implemented yet.
